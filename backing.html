How it's calculated

The backing has to be bigger than the quilt top so it can be loaded on a longarm frame or pinned flat without running short. The usual rule is 4" extra on every side, so the backing is 8" wider and 8" longer than the quilt. Some longarm quilters ask for 6"; check with yours and change the "extra" field.

backing size = (width + 2 × extra) by (length + 2 × extra)

panels = smallest number where panels × fabric width − 1/2" per seam ≥ size to cover

fabric = panels × panel length, rounded up to the next 1/8 yard

When the backing is wider than your fabric, you sew panels together. There are two ways to do it. With vertical seams, panels hang side by side across the width and each one is as long as the backing. With horizontal seams, panels are stacked down the length and each one is as long as the backing is wide. The calculator works out both and recommends the one that needs less fabric. Each joining seam takes 1/2" (1/4" from each panel).

Worked example: a 60" × 72" quilt needs a 68" × 80" backing. Two 42" panels cover 83 1/2", which is enough either way. Vertical seams need two panels 80" long, 160" or 4 1/2 yards. Horizontal seams need two panels 68" long, 136" or 3 7/8 yards, the better choice. With 108" wide backing a single 68" piece, turned so the 108" width runs down the quilt, covers it: 2 yards and no seams.

For anything queen size and up, wide backing usually saves both fabric and piecing time, because three panels of 42" fabric get long and heavy. Your yardage is rounded up to the next 1/8 yard; meters are rounded up to the next 0.1 m.

Frequently asked questions

How much bigger than the quilt top should the backing be?

Plan on 4 inches extra on each side, 8 inches total in each direction. Longarm quilters often ask for 4 to 6 inches per side, so ask before you cut.

Is 108 inch wide backing worth it?

For queen and king quilts it usually is. You avoid two long seams and often buy less total yardage. For baby and throw quilts, standard 42 inch fabric with one seam is often cheaper.

Should backing seams run horizontally or vertically?

Both are fine for the finished quilt. Pick the layout that uses less fabric. Many longarm quilters prefer seams running parallel to the rollers, so check with yours if you are sending the quilt out.

What width should I enter for 44 inch fabric?

Use the usable width after removing selvages, usually 40 to 42 inches. If you are not sure, enter 40 to be safe.
