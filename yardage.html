How it's calculated

Quilters cut patches from strips that run across the width of fabric (WOF), selvage to selvage. So the question is: how many pieces fit across one strip, and how many strips do you need?

pieces per strip = WOF ÷ piece side, rounded down

strips = pieces needed ÷ pieces per strip, rounded up

fabric = strips × strip width (+ extra %), rounded up to 1/8 yard

For rectangles, there are two ways to cut: strips as wide as the short side with the long side across the WOF, or the other way round. The calculator tries both and picks the one that uses less fabric. This is the same packing logic QuiltCraft Pro uses for its cutting lists.

Worked example: forty 5" squares. 42 ÷ 5 = 8.4, so 8 squares fit across a strip. 40 ÷ 8 = 5 strips, each 5" wide, which is 25" of fabric, or 0.69 yard, rounded up to 3/4 yard. Add 10% for mistakes and it becomes 27 1/2", or 7/8 yard.

A rectangle example: thirty 2 1/2" × 4 1/2" pieces. Cutting 4 1/2" strips and sub-cutting at 2 1/2" gives 16 per strip, so 2 strips, 9" of fabric. Cutting 2 1/2" strips gives only 9 per strip and needs 4 strips (10"). The first wins: 1/4 yard.

Enter cut sizes (including seam allowance), or tick the box to enter finished sizes and let the calculator add 1/2". For half square triangles, use the cut square size from the HST calculator. Meters are rounded up to the next 0.1 m.

Frequently asked questions

What is the usable width of fabric?

Quilting cotton is sold 42 to 44 inches wide, but after trimming the selvages you can count on about 40 to 42 inches. Enter 40 if you want a safety buffer.

Should I buy extra fabric?

Yes, a little. Adding 10 percent covers a miscut, shrinkage if you prewash and straightening the fabric edge. Directional prints may need more.

Can I use this for fat quarters?

Yes. A fat quarter is about 18 x 21 inches. Enter 21 as the width; the number of strips tells you how many 21 inch strips you need, and every fat quarter gives about 18 inches of strip height.

Does this include seam allowance?

It uses the sizes you type as cut sizes. Tick the finished-size box to have 1/2 inch added to each side length automatically.
