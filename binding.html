How it's calculated

Binding goes all the way around the quilt, so the starting point is the perimeter: twice the width plus twice the length. You then need a little more for the diagonal seams that join the strips, the folds at the four mitered corners, and the tail you overlap when the two ends meet. Most quilters add 10 to 15 inches; 15" is the safe default here.

binding length = 2 × (width + length) + extra

strips = binding length ÷ WOF, rounded up

fabric = strips × strip width, rounded up to the next 1/8 yard

Worked example: a 60" × 72" quilt has a perimeter of 2 × (60 + 72) = 264". Add 15" and you need 279" of binding. With 42" of usable width of fabric, 279 ÷ 42 = 6.64, so you cut 7 strips. Seven 2 1/2" strips use 17 1/2" of fabric, which is 0.49 yards. Rounded up to the next eighth that is 1/2 yard (about 0.5 m).

Why use the usable width rather than the label width? Bolts are sold as 42–44" wide, but once you trim the selvages and square up, 40–42" is what you really get. If your fabric is narrow, or you lose length to the 45° joining seams, enter 40 and the calculator will add a strip if needed.

The strip width sets how wide the finished binding is. A 2 1/2" double-fold strip sewn with a 1/4" seam gives a binding that shows a bit under 3/8" on each side, which suits most cotton quilts. Narrower 2 1/4" strips give a tighter look; thick or wool batting needs 2 3/4" or more. The yardage shown is for straight-grain strips cut across the width of fabric. Meters are rounded up to the next 0.1 m.

Frequently asked questions

How wide should I cut quilt binding strips?

2 1/2" is the most common width for double-fold binding. Use 2 1/4" for a slimmer binding on thin batting and 2 3/4" to 3" for thick, high-loft or wool batting.

How much extra binding should I add?

Add 10 to 15 inches to the perimeter. That covers the diagonal joining seams, the mitered corners and the overlap where the ends are joined. If you are new to binding, use the full 15 inches.

How many binding strips for a queen quilt?

A 90 x 108 queen quilt has a 396 inch perimeter. With 15 inches extra that is 411 inches, so you need 10 strips of 42 inch fabric, which is 25 inches of fabric, or 3/4 yard when cut at 2 1/2 inches.

Do I need bias binding?

Only for curved, scalloped or rounded edges. Straight edges are fine with crosswise straight-grain strips, which is what this calculator assumes. Bias binding takes more fabric and is cut differently.
